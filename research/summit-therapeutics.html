SMMT: HARMONi-2 clears the Keytruda bar in China; the Western question is still open

Post-WCLC review · NEUTRAL VIEW · Price ~$17.85 (approx., 20 Sep 2026) [28] · Market cap ~$14bn

Date: 24 September 2026 · Analyst: Chi Leong Andy Fu

Bottom line

WCLC delivered the best week of data ivonescimab has had. Akeso's HARMONi-2 became the first randomized Phase III to show an overall survival (OS) win against pembrolizumab (Keytruda) [2]. Summit's global HARMONi trial now has a survival confidence interval that no longer crosses 1 [3].

Neither result answers the question Summit's value depends on: does the benefit hold in Western patients, with chemotherapy on board, against pembrolizumab plus chemotherapy? Only Summit's HARMONi-3 can answer that, and its squamous cohort already missed an interim look in April [12].

After a ~44% move in 30 days [28], much of the good news is in the price. We stay Neutral into the HARMONi-3 squamous final PFS. The thresholds that would move us are in Section 4.

Company background

What Summit is. Summit Therapeutics (Nasdaq: SMMT) was founded in 2003 and is headquartered in Miami [1]. It began as an antibiotics developer. In July 2026 it sold its last legacy asset, ridinilazole, to Biossil [4], leaving a single-asset oncology company with no approved product and no revenue.

Chairman and co-CEO Robert Duggan controls the company with a stake near 75% [11]. Duggan previously ran Pharmacyclics and sold it to AbbVie for $21bn in 2015 [11].

The asset. Ivonescimab (SMT112 in Summit's territories, AK112 elsewhere) was engineered by China's Akeso [1]. It is a bispecific antibody that blocks two targets in one molecule: - PD-1, the immune checkpoint Keytruda targets, which releases the brakes on the immune response. - VEGF, which tumours use to grow blood vessels and to suppress immune cells.

Summit describes the four-arm (tetravalent) design as binding PD-1 more strongly where VEGF is present. That is meant to concentrate activity in tumour tissue rather than healthy tissue [1]. The investment thesis is that this could replace Keytruda-based regimens, starting in lung cancer.

The Akeso deal. Summit in-licensed ivonescimab in December 2022 for $500m upfront, in a deal worth up to $5bn [8][10]. Akeso receives low-double-digit royalties on sales [9]. Summit's territories are the US, Canada, Europe and Japan, expanded in June 2024 to Latin America, the Middle East and Africa [8]. Akeso keeps China and runs its own China-only trials. This split matters for reading the data: Akeso's trials are in Chinese patients, while Summit's are global and include Western patients.

Where things stand.

  • Akeso (China): ivonescimab is approved in three indications:
  • second-line EGFR-mutated NSCLC (May 2024) [1]
  • first-line PD-L1-positive NSCLC as monotherapy (April 2025) [2]
  • first-line squamous NSCLC (August 2026) [16]
  • Summit (its own territories): the first US filing covers second-line EGFR-mutated NSCLC. The FDA accepted it in January 2026 and has set a decision date (PDUFA) of 14 November 2026 [1].

Across both companies there are 15 Phase III studies, 4 of them run by Summit globally [1].

1. HARMONi-2: what it changes

Until September, the fair criticism of the whole class was that no PD-(L)1 x VEGF bispecific had shown a survival benefit over Keytruda. HARMONi-2 ends that. Ivonescimab alone gave patients a median 30.8 months against 22.6 months on pembrolizumab alone, and the result was statistically significant [2].

Four things follow:

  • The mechanism is validated against the right comparator. This is no longer a PFS-only story.
  • HARMONi-7 looks materially de-risked. It tests the same thing, ivonescimab monotherapy vs pembrolizumab monotherapy, in PD-L1 ≥50% patients, where HARMONi-2 showed its strongest effect (HR 0.58) [2].
  • The whole class re-rates, not just Summit. Large pharma already owns rival assets:
  • BMS paid $3.5bn upfront to co-develop BioNTech's PD-L1 x VEGF bispecific [26].
  • Pfizer paid $1.25bn upfront for 3SBio's candidate [25].
  • Merck paid $588m upfront for LaNova's LM-299 [24].
  • AbbVie paid $650m upfront for RemeGen's RC148 in January 2026 [23].

HARMONi-2 is evidence for all of them. Summit's lead is time, not exclusivity. - Partnering leverage improves. Bloomberg reported in 2025 that AstraZeneca discussed a licence worth up to $15bn [11]. No deal was announced, but an OS win against Keytruda strengthens Summit's hand in any future talks.

2. HARMONi-2: what it doesn't change

The result is from China only, from an interim analysis, on a secondary endpoint, using monotherapy, with data generated and analysed by Akeso [3]. Each qualifier matters for Summit specifically:

  • It can't carry a US filing on its own. In 2022, the FDA's oncology advisory committee voted 14–1 that sintilimab needed additional trials applicable to US patients before a decision. Its application rested on a trial run only in China [31]. HARMONi-2 is supporting evidence for the FDA, not a basis for approval.
  • Monotherapy is not HARMONi-3. HARMONi-3 adds chemotherapy to both arms [15]. Pembrolizumab plus chemotherapy is a much stronger control than pembrolizumab alone, so the room for a relative benefit shrinks.
  • The weaker subgroups are the ones HARMONi-3 depends on. HARMONi-3 enrols all PD-L1 levels, and non-squamous is its larger cohort [15]. In HARMONi-2, both the PD-L1 1–49% and non-squamous intervals cross 1 [2].
  • Every OS win so far is Chinese:
  • HARMONi-A [20]
  • HARMONi-2 [2]
  • HARMONi-6 [19]
  • HARMONi-GI1, in biliary cancer [17]

The only randomized Western data is HARMONi, where the survival result has improved but remains statistically unproven [1].

3. HARMONi: better, not finished

Source: Summit press releases [1][3].

The Western hazard ratio has moved from 0.98 to 0.84 to 0.76 as follow-up lengthened from 9.2 to 23.2 months. It now matches the Asian result [1], and the full-trial interval excludes 1 [3]. That is genuinely positive for the 14 November FDA decision.

Two caveats keep it from being decisive. First, the Western interval (0.52–1.10) still includes no benefit [3]. Second, the later cuts were run after the primary analysis had missed [1], with no statistical allowance for re-testing. How the FDA weighs a clear PFS win, a narrowly missed OS endpoint and supportive but unplanned updates is the real PDUFA question.

We read this as a better approval setup than six months ago, not a certain one.

4. The readout that decides the stock

On 30 April, Summit disclosed that the HARMONi-3 squamous cohort missed its interim PFS analysis [12]. Shares opened down ~24% [13] from a prior close near $21 [14].

There are two readings:

  • Benign. The interim bar was set very high, and Summit said so. Citi estimated only ~0.001 alpha was spent on the interim [12].
  • Less benign. The effect is smaller outside China and against pembrolizumab plus chemotherapy.

China's squamous data are strong. HARMONi-6 showed a ~40% PFS advantage over tislelizumab plus chemotherapy [12] and a significant OS benefit [19]. HARMONi-2's squamous subgroup had an OS HR of 0.65 [2]. That makes the global miss harder to dismiss, not easier.

Our base case: a statistically significant win at the final analysis, but with a smaller effect than in China. We expect a PFS HR nearer 0.75–0.80 than HARMONi-6's ~0.60. A benefit as large as China's would very likely have cleared even a strict interim bar.

6. Is the good news priced in?

Largely, yes. The stock has risen ~44% over the 30 days to ~20 September, to ~$17.85 [28]. It sits a little below the ~$21 it traded at before the April miss [14], so the market has mostly forgiven the interim result on the strength of the China data.

At ~$14bn, investors are not paying for the second-line EGFR label, which is a small population. They are paying for a meaningful chance that ivonescimab takes a large share of first-line lung cancer from Keytruda, a franchise that sold $31.7bn in FY2025 across all indications [21]. That outcome depends on HARMONi-3, which has not reported.

Sell-side views are split. Jefferies upgraded to Buy with a $25 target ahead of the HARMONi-3 readout [29], while Barclays remains at Hold [30].

View: Neutral. The data are better than they were a month ago, but the stock has moved with them, and the ATM is supplying new shares into the strength.

  • We turn positive on a squamous final PFS HR ≤0.70 with a Western subgroup pointing the same way.
  • We turn negative on a squamous miss, a benefit confined to Asian sites, or a CRL citing lack of significant OS.
